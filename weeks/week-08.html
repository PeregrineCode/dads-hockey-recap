Weekly Recap

Week 8 Recap (2026-11-16 — 2026-11-22)

Tuesday, November 24, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Fortune Favors The Boldy beat Ducks on the Pond 🦆 7-2-0, and Monday gave no hint of it. The Ducks opened up 7-1-1 and held a 7-2-0 lead on Wednesday. Then the week turned. Thursday and Friday sat at 4-4-1, and Saturday put Fortune Favors The Boldy in front for good. Jim's club finished with 62 hits to 22, 38 points to 29, and 15 goals to 12. Seven of nine categories after trailing by seven is the kind of comeback that gets a banner in the rafters.

Nette's Sweaty Yetis beat Dying on this Hill 7-2-0 in a different kind of wild week. Dying on this Hill led 6-0-3 on Monday, and Nette's Sweaty Yetis answered Tuesday by taking all nine categories. The Yetis kept the lead the rest of the way. They finished with 36 assists and 47 points, and their 11 goals were enough to take that category too. Dying on this Hill held on to hits (52 to 43) and blocks (34 to 26), but that's all it kept.

50 in '07 beat Pasta La Vista 7-1-1, and the box score hides a scare. Derek's team led Monday, then spent Tuesday and Wednesday looking up at Pasta La Vista, which was up 6-2-1 on Wednesday. By Thursday the lead had changed back, and it never changed again. 50 in '07 finished with 90 shots, 37 blocks and a .905 save percentage. Pasta La Vista came away with the hit category (45 to 34) and a tie at two wins apiece.

Gavin Mackinaw beat LL Cooley J 6-3-0 with the strangest line of the week. Gavin Mackinaw scored 6 goals to 15, and still won because 26 assists, 38 blocks and 4 goalie wins carried the other categories. LL Cooley J led Tuesday and Wednesday, but its goaltending slid to a 3.45 GAA and a .869 save percentage. Jesse's club took the lead back Thursday and kept it. Winning on defence and goaltending is old-time hockey, and I approve.

Hacklin Celeweenie beat Loosy Kuchy 5-4-0 on goaltending and grit. Loosy Kuchy led Monday, Hacklin Celeweenie went in front Tuesday, and after a 4-4-1 tie on Wednesday Mac's club took the lead back for good. Mac's club won with a 1.97 GAA and a .915 save percentage, plus 55 hits to 35 and 4 wins. Loosy Kuchy put up 39 points and 15 goals and still came up one category short, which is a hard way to lose.

Busting Out beat On The Bench Hockey 2.0 6-3-0 by volume. Ivan's team fired 101 shots to 78 and led in hits, blocks, wins, GAA and save percentage. On The Bench Hockey 2.0 took goals, assists and points, which was only the offensive side of the ledger. The goalie categories decided it.

Oscar Meier Wiener and Jetting out of Winnipeg finished tied 4-4-1. They split goals at 11 apiece. Oscar Meier Wiener took assists, points, hits and blocks, while Jetting out of Winnipeg took shots, wins, GAA and save percentage. Nobody wins a tie, but Dave's skaters can at least say they did their part, since the 4.03 GAA and .863 save percentage were the whole problem.

Players of the Week

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Jake Neighbours of Nette's Sweaty Yetis takes the skater crown, and he earned it in every corner of the scoresheet. One goal, eight assists, nine points, nine shots and eleven hits. That's a winger who plays the way the old Blues teams were built, with his nose in the dirty areas and his stick on the puck. Playmaking from a guy who throws his body around like that is a rare combination, and STL's top line fed him all week. His teammate Robert Thomas also put up 10 points on two goals and eight assists, which means Nette's Sweaty Yetis got a pair of Blues doing the heavy lifting on one roster.

The runners-up were loud. Tage Thompson of Hacklin Celeweenie fired 16 shots and scored five goals, the kind of volume shooting that makes a goalie's week miserable, and he still found two assists on top. Brock Boeser of Dying on this Hill piled up seven assists and ten shots with eight hits, which is more physical than Boeser usually gets credit for. Thomas's ten points were the highest total among the four skaters on this list, and Neighbours' edge in hits (11 to 2) and shots (9 to 8) is what kept Thomas from the top spot.

In goal, Jake Oettinger of Jetting out of Winnipeg wins it on workload. Ninety shots against, 84 saves, three wins and a .933 save percentage with a 1.94 GAA. That's a goalie who stood in the crease while DAL got peppered and never blinked. Andrei Vasilevskiy of Busting Out had the sharper rate numbers, a 1.50 GAA and .940 save percentage with two wins on 50 shots, and Ilya Sorokin of Nette's Sweaty Yetis was right there at 1.51 and .939. Linus Ullmark of Hacklin Celeweenie rounds out the group at 1.50 and .929. Any of the three could have taken it on a lighter week, but Oettinger's three wins and the sheer pile of saves carry the day.

Power Rankings

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Contenders

The top of the board is a four-team scrum, and every one of them has a different problem. Three of the four won this week, and the one that lost still owns the best winning percentage in the league.

#1 50 in '07 — 41-24-7 (.618) — W, 7-1-1

Derek's crew took seven categories, tied wins and dropped only hits for a 7-1-1 beating. That is the kind of week a contender is supposed to have, and 90 shots and 37 blocks did the dirty work.

#2 Fortune Favors The Boldy — 43-24-5 (.632) — W, 7-2-0

Jim's club piled up a 7-2-0 win and has the second-best winning percentage in the building. Sixty-two hits, the most in the league, will wear down anybody, and this club did it while trailing by seven categories early in the week.

#3 On The Bench Hockey 2.0 — 47-20-5 (.688) — L, 3-6-0

Jordan took a 3-6-0 loss, and I still can't drop a team with 47 wins. The .688 mark is the best in the league and one bad week doesn't erase it. Call it a bad night in a long season.

#4 Gavin Mackinaw — 39-32-1 (.549) — W, 6-3-0

Jesse's group took a 6-3-0 decision on blocks, hits and goaltending. The record says average, the roster says dangerous.

Solid

Respectable hockey from three teams who can beat anybody and lose to everybody. This tier is one hot goalie away from a promotion or a demotion.

#5 Pasta La Vista — 41-25-6 (.611) — L, 1-7-1

Tyler got flattened 1-7-1, a week that belongs in a hockey tragedy anthology. The .611 record is why they only slide a few spots and not off a cliff. One category out of nine is the kind of number that makes a manager stare at the ceiling.

#6 Hacklin Celeweenie — 36-31-5 (.535) — W, 5-4-0

Mac eked out a 5-4-0 win, the narrowest margin there is in a nine-category week. Ugly or not, it goes in the win column.

#7 LL Cooley J — 38-30-4 (.556) — L, 3-6-0

Dennis dropped a 3-6-0 decision, and the defending champion needs more than that against the teams above. The record is fine, but a three-peat isn't built on weeks like this. Time to tighten the lineup.

Mediocre

Four teams who spent the week treading water, and two of them literally tied.

#8 Nette's Sweaty Yetis — 26-37-9 (.424) — W, 7-2-0

Andrew's Yetis rolled to a 7-2-0 win, the best result of anyone in this tier. The record still has too many losses to buy a ticket up. Still, nobody else in the bottom half looked this healthy.

#9 Loosy Kuchy — 34-37-1 (.479) — L, 4-5-0

Matthew lost 4-5-0, one category from a tie or a win. That's the story of a roster stuck on the wrong side of a coin flip. A .479 club has to start winning those.

#10 Oscar Meier Wiener — 31-36-5 (.465) — T, 4-4-1

Dave's team tied 4-4-1 and left the building with nothing resolved. Nine categories, four apiece, one dead heat. A draw is a draw and the standings don't care how you feel about it.

#11 Jetting out of Winnipeg — 30-40-2 (.431) — T, 4-4-1

Cole also settled for a 4-4-1 tie, which keeps the club exactly where it was. Ten categories under .500 on the season is a hole, but a tie at least doesn't dig it deeper. Cole needs a win soon.

Rebuilding

The bottom three are playing for pride. Pride and a good look at next year's auction.

#12 Busting Out — 24-43-5 (.368) — W, 6-3-0

Ivan's club pulled out a 6-3-0 win and climbs a spot for it. This is the first sign of a pulse. Good to see the lights on.

#13 Ducks on the Pond 🦆 — 23-46-3 (.340) — L, 2-7-0

Tom's club followed last week's win with a 2-7-0 loss, and one goalie start all week cost them both ratio categories. The ducks are sitting.

#14 Dying on this Hill — 22-50-0 (.306) — L, 2-7-0

Jean-Christophe lost 2-7-0 and owns the worst winning percentage in the league at .306. Fifty category losses is a number that shouldn't be said out loud. The name of the team has never been more accurate.

Movers and Shakers

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Tyler and Dennis still sit in the playoff picture, but a team that keeps drifting down the board eventually runs out of room to drift. Best Pickup of the Week

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Sam Rinzel takes the top spot, and 50 in '07 gets the credit for it. The young Chicago defenceman played three games in the active lineup and put up a goal, four assists, ten shots on goal, three hits and five blocks. That is a defenceman filling every column of the box score, and a blocked-shot line like that is the kind of work I spent 400 minor-league games doing for free. Five points from the blue line with ten shots and five blocks on the side is the best free-agent grab of the week.

Loosy Kuchy has a fair gripe at number two. Elias Lindholm of BOS banged home four goals and added an assist in three games, with nine shots and two blocks. Five points apiece, but Lindholm did it with a pile of goals. Hacklin Celeweenie takes third with Martin Pospisil of CGY, who delivered 13 hits and six shots across three games to go with a goal and an assist. Thirteen hits is a hitter's week, and I'll always have a soft spot for that.

Hall of Shame

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Mason McTavish of STL leads the Hall of Shame, and the blame belongs to On The Bench Hockey 2.0. Three games with the team, two of them in the lineup, and the final tally is one assist, one shot, one hit and nothing else. Then comes the real gem: McTavish scored a goal and blocked two shots on a day he sat on the bench. The manager owned a player who produced on the one day he wasn't playing him. That takes a special kind of timing.

Busting Out gets the headline treatment for Erik Karlsson of PIT. Eight shots on goal and three blocks across three games in the active lineup, and not one point. Karlsson is a former Norris winner and a man who once put up 101 points from the back end, but that player did not show up in this week's box score. He cost $84 of a $100 budget. I'll get to the money in the Transaction Desk, but a defenceman who generates shots and no points for that price is the kind of thing that keeps a manager up at night.

Hacklin Celeweenie rounds out the list with Dmitry Orlov of SJ. Three assists in two games is fine on paper, but he added one shot and nothing in hits or blocks. A Hall of Shame entry on a three-point week is a fair complaint only because the competition for pickups was stiffer than that. Orlov is a veteran defenceman with a long track record, and this was a quiet visit to the box score.

Goalie Stream of the Week

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Joonas Korpisalo of NYR is the stream of the week, and Ducks on the Pond 🦆 gets the credit for it. One start, one win, one goal against and 24 saves on 25 shots for a 1.00 GAA and a .960 save percentage. That is the line every manager hopes for when they roll the dice on a streamer. The catch is that it was the Ducks' only goalie game of the week, one short of the league's two-game minimum, so the 1.00 and .960 never counted and both ratio categories went to the other side.

Loosy Kuchy gets the silver with Brandon Bussi of CAR. One win, two goals against, 25 saves on 27 shots, a 2.00 GAA and a .926 save percentage. That is a clean, professional night in the crease, even in a week Loosy Kuchy lost the wins category 1-4. Both managers took a shot on one start and walked away with a win.

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

The bench is where the damage was done this week, and Jetting out of Winnipeg leads the list. Matthew Knies sat on two days when he had stats, and the week's line for those days was two goals, three assists, five points, nine shots and five hits. Five points and nine shots from a player sitting on the bench is a lot of production to leave in the stands. JJ Peterka was benched on another day with a goal, two shots and a block. Two benched skaters with a combined goal-and-points haul is a lineup-card problem, and the manager has to own it.

The rest of the list: Hacklin Celeweenie left Taylor Hall on the bench on two days when he produced a goal, an assist and three shots. On The Bench Hockey 2.0 benched Mason McTavish on a day he had a goal, a shot and two blocks, which makes the Hall of Shame entry even better. Oscar Meier Wiener had Jack McBain on the bench for eight hits on a day he had stats. Oscar Meier Wiener won hits anyway, 46 to 42, but eight hits sitting next to the water bottles is still wasted work.

Transaction Desk

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

The big spender this week was Busting Out, who put $84 on Erik Karlsson of PIT and dropped Darnell Nurse to make room. That is 84% of a $100 budget on a defenceman who posted eight shots, three blocks and no points. The bid leaves Busting Out at $0 remaining with a long season ahead. It is hard to see that spend as justified, and the Hall of Shame has already said what it thinks of the return.

The smaller moves were more sensible. On The Bench Hockey 2.0 bid $7 on Vince Dunn of SEA and dropped Dan Vladar, getting an assist, two shots and a block. Pasta La Vista spent $5 on Tristan Luneau of ANA for five shots, a hit and two blocks, and still has $95 to work with. Loosy Kuchy put in a $3 bid on Brock Nelson of COL, dropping Jordan Kyrou, and sits at $0 remaining. Total spending came to $99 on the week.

On the war-chest side, 50 in '07, Hacklin Celeweenie, Oscar Meier Wiener, Jetting out of Winnipeg, Nette's Sweaty Yetis and Dying on this Hill all hold the full $100. Gavin Mackinaw has $44 and LL Cooley J has $47. Fortune Favors The Boldy is also at $0, and Ducks on the Pond 🦆 is down to $7. Three teams are out of budget entirely. They can still grab free agents at $0, but anyone on waivers is out of their reach.

The Fancy Stats Desk

by Dr. Ingrid Lindqvist

Fortune Favors The Boldy led the league in hits (62), shared the goals lead (15) with Loosy Kuchy and LL Cooley J, and beat Ducks on the Pond 🦆 7-2-0. That sounds ordinary until the goalie lines come up. The Ducks posted the best GAA (1.00) and the best SV% (.960) in the league and won neither category. Korpisalo's single start was their only goalie game of the week, one short of the league's two-game minimum, so both ratio categories went to Fortune Favors The Boldy at 3.51 and .872. The Ducks' two wins were shots (77 to 76) and blocks (36 to 27). They had 22 hits, the league low, and 29 points against 38. The best goaltending rates in the league were worth nothing because there was only one game of them. Wins count as their own category, and Fortune Favors The Boldy took it 3 to 1.

Nette's Sweaty Yetis show where points can come from. They led the league with 36 assists and 47 points, and they beat Dying on this Hill 7-2-0. Their goals were 11, a little below the 11.64 league average. All of the points edge came from assists, which ran well above the 20.86 average. Assists are the least stable of the scoring inputs, so I would expect that total to fall back toward the average. Gavin Mackinaw is the mirror image. Their 6 goals were the league low, and they still won 6-3-0 over LL Cooley J, whose 15 goals tied for the league high. Gavin Mackinaw won through 4 wins, 38 blocks and 46 hits. Those are the categories that need no finishing.

The tie between Oscar Meier Wiener and Jetting out of Winnipeg (4-4-1) is the odd one. Oscar Meier Wiener had the league's worst GAA (4.03) and the second-worst SV% (.863), ahead of only Dying on this Hill (.856). They still came away with a tie, because their skaters outscored Jetting out of Winnipeg 28 points to 22. That 22 was the league low. Both teams had 11 goals and the gap came entirely from assists, 17 to 11. A goalie line that bad rarely leaves a team level, and here it was one category short of a loss.

Busting Out led the league with 101 shots on goal against a league average of 82.07, and they won 6-3-0, but their goals (12) were barely above the 11.64 average. Volume of shots is not the same as volume of goals. A team can lead the league in shots and score at the league rate, and this was that week. I would not expect those shots to turn into goals at that rate over a longer stretch. Their .916 save percentage was the more stable part of the win.
