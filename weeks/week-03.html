Week 3 Recap (2026-10-12 — 2026-10-18)

Monday, October 19, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Mackin' a Drai Hard edged Jetting out of Winnipeg 5-4-0, and it's a gutsy result for a team that got no goaltending at all. Zero wins, a 4.33 GAA and a .841 save percentage would sink most teams. Matthew's skaters won assists, points, shots, hits and blocks, 21 assists to 17, 84 shots to 78, 39 hits to 34 and 39 blocks to 27, and that carried the week. Cole's side took goals (10 to 9), wins, GAA and save percentage on a 2.03 and a .927. Cole did everything a goalie tandem can do and still lost. Matthew won it on his skaters alone, the honest way, and Muzz will take that every time.

Fortune Favors The Boldy took down 50 in '07 7-2-0, and the daily results had Fortune Favors The Boldy out in front from the first day to the last. Jim's group put up 39 points, 98 shots and 60 hits, and four goalie wins sealed the rest. Derek's goalies did hold up their end. 50 in '07 won GAA at 2.17 to 2.56 and save percentage at .922 to .906. That's a lot of goalie to carry and not enough skater to go with it.

Oscar Meier Wiener beat Faulk me Sideways 7-1-1, a score that hides how thin the margin was in the crease. The goalie wins tied at three apiece. Faulk's only category was GAA at 2.21 to 2.24, and Dave took save percentage at .924 to .904. The difference came up front: 7 goals to 3, 87 shots to 76 and 46 hits to 29. Oscar Meier Wiener led from the first day and never let go.

Jacks in the Box handled Nette's Sweaty Yetis 7-1-1 on the back of a 103-shot week. All that rubber turned into 12 goals and 34 points, and a .912 save percentage closed the deal. Andrew's side won assists, 24 to 22, and tied wins at two each. After that it was all Jacks in the Box, and Dennis led throughout. I cover this league as a neutral third party, and even I have to admit that was clean.

Hacklin Celeweenie blasted Ducks on the Pond 8-1-0 and never trailed on any day. Mac's goalies won four games with a 1.96 GAA and a .920 save percentage, and the skaters added 55 hits and 37 blocks. Tom's Ducks took assists, 17 to 16, and that's the lone thing that kept this from a shutout. Pasta La Vista beat Locks and Hagels 6-3-0 by winning the goalie categories (3 wins, a 2.27 GAA and a .920 save percentage) against a 4.12 and a .852. Jean-Christophe's squad took shots, hits and blocks with 86, 58 and 45, an honest-work week that the goaltending sank. Tyler's goal margin, 17 to 15, was thin, but it counted.

On The Bench Hockey 2.0 finished off Gavin Mackinaw 8-1-0, and the daily results had Jordan ahead all week. The skaters did it all, with 41 points, 30 assists, 101 shots and 43 blocks. The goaltending was ugly (a 3.49 GAA and a .863 save percentage), but Jesse's was worse at 4.29 and a .876 save percentage, so the GAA still went to Jordan. Gavin Mackinaw's only category was save percentage, and that's a long way to come to be a footnote.

Players of the Week

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Sam Reinhart of Jacks in the Box takes the skater crown, and Dennis can enjoy it with a straight face because the numbers don't care who signs the cheque. Five goals, three assists, eight points and 17 shots on goal for the Florida centre. Reinhart has long been the quiet engine on that FLA power play, the guy who never gets the headline while others get the highlight reel, and this week he took the headline for himself. Seventeen shots is a lot of rubber going toward the net, and five of them found twine. That's a beauty of a week.

The runners-up are a murderers' row. Matthew Tkachuk of Faulk me Sideways piled up 10 assists for 10 points from the FLA wing, proving that when his line is cooking, the puck finds his stick before he's even asked for it, and he added eight shots and two hits for flavour. Kirill Kaprizov of 50 in '07 scored four goals and added three assists for seven points on 18 shots out of MIN, the most shots of this group. Then there's Moritz Seider of On The Bench Hockey 2.0, my kind of player: a Detroit defenceman with 11 blocks, eight hits, six shots and three assists. He gave this league a week of honest, bone-rattling hockey from the blue line, and it's the hits and blocks that make me love him.

Between the pipes, Ilya Sorokin of Nette's Sweaty Yetis is the goalie of the week. Two wins, a 1.50 GAA and a .945 save percentage, with 52 saves on 55 shots for the NYI. That's a clinic. The Yetis' manager Andrew gets a goalie standing on his head and a week of wins out of it, and a goalie who gives up three goals all week is a manager's best friend. Jeremy Swayman of Oscar Meier Wiener was right behind with two wins, a 2.00 GAA and a .938 save percentage on 60 saves from 64 shots. Mackenzie Blackwood of Pasta La Vista went 54 for 58 for a .931, and Pyotr Kochetkov of Fortune Favors The Boldy rounded out the podium with a .911 and two wins of his own, 41 stops on 45 shots. Four goalies, four pairs of wins, and not a bad week among them.

Transaction Desk

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

The big story is the trade that got hijacked. Jacks in the Box and Jetting out of Winnipeg had agreed to a deal on October 12 that would have sent Logan Cooley to Winnipeg and Aleksander Barkov to Dennis. On October 15, a counter offer from Mackin' a Drai Hard overturned it. Matthew's offer landed John Tavares in Winnipeg and Barkov on his own roster. Cole ends up with Tavares, who landed on IR just hours after the deal went through. Tough luck for Cole. Matthew gets Barkov, a long-term IR stash, and I give this one to Matthew, because he got the prize piece. Cole, for his part, took the better offer when it came, so he comes out of it fine too. Dennis got outbid, and he keeps Cooley.

On the FAAB side, the headline is Fortune Favors The Boldy and the full $100 on Joel Eriksson Ek. Jim now has $0 left for the rest of the season, and with a maximum of four adds a week, that is a thin cushion if a goalie goes down. Eriksson Ek's three assists, eight shots and seven hits make the opening bid look justified for one week, but a zero balance in October is a gamble. Free agents who are not on waivers still cost nothing, so Jim isn't locked out of the wire, but he can't win any bidding war from here on.

Mackin' a Drai Hard also went deep, bidding $72 on William Eklund of OTT, and now has $3 remaining. Between that and the Barkov deal, Matthew has gone all in on the early season. Ducks on the Pond 🦆 has $7 left after a $4 bid on Luca Cagnoni, so Tom is nearly tapped out as well. Three teams have now burned through almost their entire budget in the first weeks.

At the other end, Jacks in the Box spent $5 on Zach Benson and has $63 remaining, and Faulk me Sideways has $85 left after the $15 on DeBrusk. Nine teams still sit at the full $100 and made no bids. The league spent $196 this week, and four of the five bidders are already nearly out of ammunition or well down the road. Benson sat on Dennis's bench all week, which does not make that $5 look like a bargain.

The Fancy Stats Desk

by Dr. Ingrid Lindqvist

The week's cleanest oddity belongs to Locks and Hagels. They finished with the league high in blocks (45) and 58 hits, behind only the 60 from Fortune Favors The Boldy. Their 86 shots on goal also beat their opponent's 85. They still lost 6-3-0 to Pasta La Vista. Their 15 goals were the second-highest total in the league, and only Pasta La Vista's 17 beat it. The goalies finished the job: zero wins, a 4.12 GAA and an .852 save percentage against a league average of .896. Jean-Christophe's skaters won three categories and lost goals, assists and points, and his goalies handed back all three goalie categories.

On The Bench Hockey 2.0 is the mirror image. Jordan's team posted the league's best assists (30) and points (41) and went 8-1-0. The goaltending was almost as bad as Locks and Hagels': a 3.49 GAA and an .863 save percentage, well under the .896 average. It cost them exactly one category, save percentage, to Gavin Mackinaw's .876. They won the GAA category anyway because Gavin Mackinaw posted a 4.29. When an opponent's goalies are that bad, a team can survive its own.

Mackin' a Drai Hard ran the same experiment the other way. Matthew's team had the league's worst GAA (4.33) and the league's worst save percentage (.841), and still went 5-4-0. The skaters won five of the six skater categories against Jetting out of Winnipeg. The only skater category they lost was goals, 10 to 9. The goalie line lost all three goalie categories, which is as far as it could go. Skater depth carried a goaltending disaster across the line, with no help from the crease.

The sharpest irony is on the other side of that matchup. Jetting out of Winnipeg had the league's best save percentage (.927) and a 2.03 GAA, and Cole's team still took the loss. A strong goalie line gives a team only three categories to win. The other six belong to the skaters, and a .927 does nothing for a team that loses assists, points, shots, hits and blocks. Goalies can't make up for a thin skater group, not when skaters own six of the nine categories.
