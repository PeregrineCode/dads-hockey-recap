Week 2 Recap (2026-10-05 — 2026-10-11)

Monday, October 12, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

The comeback of the week came out of Winnipeg. Jetting out of Winnipeg trailed Goon City Grifters every single day from Monday through Saturday, at one point down 7-2-0 from Goon City's side on Wednesday, and still walked out with a 5-4-0 win. Cole's crew only took the lead on the final day, flipping the matchup on Sunday for its one and only lead change. The goalies did the heavy lifting: a 1.00 GAA and a .957 save percentage on three wins, against a 2.00 and .895 from Goon City. Ivan's group piled up 13 goals, 26 assists and 39 points and still went home empty-handed, which is the hockey equivalent of outshooting a team 40-20 and losing 1-0. Call Cole "The Closer." A lunch-pail win, and a beauty.

Two other matchups finished 5-4-0 and might have aged me a year each. Hacklin Celeweenie edged Gavin Mackinaw with 100 shots, 52 hits, four wins, a 1.73 GAA and a .933 save percentage. Jesse's club held the lead briefly on Wednesday before Mac's boys reclaimed it and never let go. Gavin Mackinaw won goals, assists, points and blocks (53 of them), but a 3.67 GAA and .888 save percentage sank the crease. Hits settled it, 52 to 50, and that's why I preach the honest categories.

Jacks in the Box beat Fortune Favors The Boldy 5-3-1. Dennis's group won assists, points, shots (101), blocks (46) and save percentage, with wins deadlocked at one apiece. Jim's side took goals, hits (52) and GAA, but a .867 save percentage left the Boldy short. Pasta La Vista beat On The Bench Hockey 2.0 by the same 5-3-1 score, and it came down to a .886 to .882 save percentage margin. Tyler's club also hammered out 63 hits, a bruising lunch-pail effort. Jordan's bunch won goals, assists and points but couldn't convert that into the crease categories.

Mackin' a Drai Hard beat 50 in '07 6-3-0 despite a 3.61 GAA, because Matthew's group won goals, assists, points, blocks, wins and a .882 to .875 save percentage squeaker. Derek's squad had the shots (94) and an enormous 66 hits, but went winless in goal with zero wins. Nette's Sweaty Yetis beat Ducks on the Pond 🦆 6-2-1, taking goals, assists, points, shots, GAA and save percentage. Tom's flock won hits and wins, with blocks tied at 23 each, but a 3.01 GAA against a 2.00 from Andrew's Yetis told the story.

The blowout belonged to Oscar Meier Wiener, which beat Locks and Hagels 8-1-0. Dave, back after nearly ten years away, led wire to wire and hit a perfect 9-0-0 on Thursday and Friday. Jean-Christophe's side salvaged blocks, 31 to 30, and nothing else. A .833 save percentage and a 4.21 GAA is a goaltending disaster, and Locks and Hagels never had a chance. Dave's welcome-back tour is off to a strong start.

Players of the Week

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Auston Matthews gets the Skater of the Week crown, and it took only a glance at the sheet. Three goals and six assists for nine points, with 11 shots on net and four blocks thrown in for the lunch-pail crowd. That is a centre driving the play for Gavin Mackinaw, and Jesse can stop pretending the runner-up finish last spring was a fluke. Matthews has been a goal-scoring freight train in Toronto for years, and this week he added the passing too. I'm calling Jesse "Mr. Nine Points" until further notice. Tim Stutzle was the closest challenger for On The Bench Hockey 2.0: four goals, three assists, and nine hits from a skilled Ottawa centre. That is the kind of honest, all-category line this old defenceman respects. Sebastian Aho (Goon City Grifters) piled up seven points and a dozen shots for Carolina. Tage Thompson of Hacklin Celeweenie fired 22 shots and scored three times, a sniper's week from a Buffalo centre who shoots from everywhere.

Jake Oettinger takes the Goalie of the Week honour for Jetting out of Winnipeg. He stopped all 39 shots he faced, won twice, and didn't allow a goal on the week for a perfect 1.000 save percentage. Dallas has leaned on him for years, and Cole got the full return on that reputation. The sample is thin, so the heavy-workload crowd will point to the 39 shots. Still, a goalie who never gets beat is a goalie who wins you two categories before the skaters touch the puck.

The runners-up in goal were all busier. Linus Ullmark went 3-0 for Ottawa with a 1.10 GAA and a .947 save percentage on 57 shots against, a quietly dominant week for Hacklin Celeweenie. That makes Mac the only manager with two names on this page, Ullmark in goal and Thompson up front. Jeremy Swayman won twice for Oscar Meier Wiener with a .921 behind a 2.01 GAA, which is exactly what Dave drafted him to do. Andrei Vasilevskiy rounds it out for Goon City Grifters with two wins and a 2.00 GAA, though his .895 save percentage shows Tampa made him earn them.

Power Rankings

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Contenders

The top four all won this week, and none of them looked shaky doing it. This is the group that will still be standing when the bracket gets drawn in week 24.

#1 Mackin' a Drai Hard — 13-5-0 (.722) — W, 6-3-0

Matthew has never finished higher than third, and this is the best start he has put together. A 6-3-0 win keeps him atop the board, and nobody is close to his record. Lunch-pail hockey, the kind that wins in April.

#2 Oscar Meier Wiener — 11-6-1 (.639) — W, 8-1-0

Dave comes back after nearly ten years away and puts together an 8-1-0 beating like he never left. That is the most lopsided result I have on the sheet this week, and it is a beauty. Call him "The Comeback Kid" and be done with it.

#3 Pasta La Vista — 10-6-2 (.611) — W, 5-3-1

Tyler made the playoffs as a rookie and looks like he plans to do it again. A 5-3-1 win is not flashy, but a team that grinds out those weeks will be hard to dislodge. Steady as a stay-at-home defenceman with a bad knee.

#4 Jetting out of Winnipeg — 11-7-0 (.611) — W, 5-4-0

Cole squeaked by at 5-4-0, the kind of win that gets you a handshake and no style points. Wins are wins, and this is a third-season manager already sitting in the top four. I'll take the ugly ones every time.

Solid

A comfortable middle shelf of teams that can beat anyone on a good week and lose to anyone on a bad one. Two of them are sitting on 10-7-1 and wondering why they are not higher.

#5 Gavin Mackinaw — 10-7-1 (.583) — L, 4-5-0

Jesse, last year's runner-up, dropped a 4-5-0 decision and slid down the board. One category separated him from a win, and that is the cruellest kind of loss. The record says contender, so don't count him out.

#6 Nette's Sweaty Yetis — 8-7-3 (.528) — W, 6-2-1

Andrew finished 14th of 16 last season and is now above .500 with a 6-2-1 win in the books. Three ties on the year tells me this team has plenty of close calls. Good hockey, and a long way from where he was.

#7 50 in '07 — 10-7-1 (.583) — L, 3-6-0

Derek won the regular season last year and has the same record as the team ahead of him, but a 3-6-0 loss is the kind that sticks in the craw. Still chasing that first title, and weeks like this do not help. The record is good, the trend is not.

Mediocre

Four teams hovering around the .500 line, none of them able to string together the weeks that would separate them from the pack. The playoff line is close enough to see from here.

#8 Fortune Favors The Boldy — 8-7-3 (.528) — L, 3-5-1

Jim fell 3-5-1 and has three ties on the season, which is a lot of nearly-but-not-quite. The record is respectable and the team is treading water. A busy waiver week could change the story.

#9 Hacklin Celeweenie — 8-9-1 (.472) — W, 5-4-0

Mac scraped out a 5-4-0 win to stay in the conversation. The record is still under .500, but a win is a win, and this team is within reach of the playoff line. Don't write him off yet.

#10 On The Bench Hockey 2.0 — 8-8-2 (.500) — L, 3-5-1

Jordan sits at an even .500 after a 3-5-1 loss, which is about as perfectly mediocre as a record gets. Not bad enough to panic, not good enough to dream. The name is starting to feel like a scouting report.

#11 Jacks in the Box — 7-10-1 (.417) — W, 5-3-1

Dennis, going for a three-peat, is at 7-10-1 and needs a lot more weeks like this 5-3-1 win. The defending champion has real ground to make up, though the season is long. Champions have climbed out of deeper holes.

Rebuilding

Three teams at the bottom with a lot of work to do. Trade deadline is March 3, and the phones should be ringing.

#12 Goon City Grifters — 5-12-1 (.306) — L, 4-5-0

Ivan has finished in the top six in all four of his seasons, so this 5-12-1 start is a shock. A 4-5-0 loss was another one-category heartbreaker. The history says he fixes it, but the clock is ticking.

#13 Ducks on the Pond 🦆 — 4-12-2 (.278) — L, 2-6-1

Tom's all-Anaheim experiment is not working, and a 2-6-1 loss only underlines it. The first champion in league history, according to the records, is now buried near the bottom. Time to diversify the portfolio.

#14 Locks and Hagels — 4-14-0 (.222) — L, 1-8-0

Jean-Christophe took a 1-8-0 beating, a disaster of a week. At 4-14-0, the worst record in the league, it has been a long season already. The only direction left is up.

Movers and Shakers

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Mackin' a Drai Hard sits atop the standings at 13-5-0, up one spot, and Matthew is running the lunch-pail operation I respect. Oscar Meier Wiener made the real noise, though. Dave comes back after nearly ten years away and vaults six places to second at 11-6-1. Call him the Prodigal Wiener. Jetting out of Winnipeg (11-7-0) climbed one to third, and Pasta La Vista (10-6-2) jumped three to fourth. Tyler made the playoffs as a rookie last year and looks like he plans to do it again. Nette's Sweaty Yetis rose four to seventh at 8-7-3, a nice recovery for Andrew after a 14th-place finish last season.

Gavin Mackinaw slid two to fifth at 10-7-1, and Fortune Favors The Boldy and On The Bench Hockey 2.0 each gave back three spots, to eighth (8-7-3) and ninth (8-8-2). Tom built that roster almost entirely from Anaheim, and so far the flock isn't flying. Jacks in the Box climbed two to 11th at 7-10-1, and Goon City Grifters also rose two, to 12th at 5-12-1. Neither is out of this, but neither is anywhere near comfortable.

Best Pickup of the Week

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Shane Pinto gets the nod, and it comes down to the hits. The Pasta La Vista pickup went 10 hits, 5 shots and 3 assists on the week, with a block thrown in. That's a lunch-pail line from a centre, and it showed up in three categories where most waiver adds barely register in one. Tyler picked up a guy who goes into the corners and comes out with the puck, and that's the kind of move I've been preaching about for years. Call him "Corner Tyler" from here on.

Easton Cowan is the runner-up, and he'd be the winner if there were a prize for value. Mackin' a Drai Hard paid $7 for two goals, 9 shots, 4 hits and 3 blocks from the Toronto winger. Matthew got a full skater line for the price of a hot dog at the rink. The only reason he isn't on top is that Pinto's 10 hits were too beautiful to pass over.

Ben Kindel rounds out the podium for Gavin Mackinaw. He had a goal, two assists and 9 shots for Pittsburgh, and the line is good. It's a touch light on the gritty stuff, and I'm always suspicious of a pickup who does his work on shots alone. Still, a rookie centre producing like that is worth a spot in the top three.

Hall of Shame

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Adam Lowry takes the top prize for Locks and Hagels, and that's a shame, because he's usually a joy to watch. The Winnipeg centre finished the week with one hit and nothing else. No goals, no assists, no shots, no blocks. A third-line grinder is supposed to hit things, and he managed to do that exactly once. Jean-Christophe grabbed a bruiser and got a ghost.

Kent Johnson rounds out the shame for Ducks on the Pond 🦆. Two shots, one block and a whole lot of nothing from the Columbus winger. The talent is real, and that's what makes this so frustrating. Tom has been fishing for upside all week, and all he's caught is a boot.

Goalie Stream of the Week

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Ville Husso takes the crown, and he did it the old-fashioned way. The Anaheim netminder stopped 27 of 29 shots, which works out to a .931 save percentage and a 2.00 GAA, and he picked up the win. That is exactly what a stream is supposed to look like: one start, one clean line, and no damage to the ratios. Gavin Mackinaw gambled on a waiver goalie and walked away with a win and a boost to both GAA and SV%. Jesse deserves a tip of the cap.

Anthony Stolarz of Toronto is the other side of the coin for Mackin' a Drai Hard. He saved 21 of 25 for a .840 save percentage and a 3.71 GAA, though he did get the win. A win is a win, and I'll take it. But that ratio is the kind that makes managers wince, and Matthew got the one category by giving back ground in two others. That's the risk with streaming, and it's why Husso is the clear call.

William Nylander had a goal and two assists. That's two star forwards warming the pine. Cole earns the "Bench Boss" title this week, and I mean it in the worst possible way.

Honourable mentions go to Pasta La Vista, who sat Carter Hart for a 26-save win on 27 shots, and Fortune Favors The Boldy, who left Luke Evangelista on the bench for two goals. Every one of these was a lineup decision that cost somebody real points. Check the lock times, gentlemen. The puck drops whether or not the roster is set.

Transaction Desk

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

The week's one trade gave Matthew Schaefer to Jacks in the Box, and sent Thomas Harley and Brandon Hagel to Locks and Hagels. As a neutral party, I'll say this: Schaefer is an elite young defenceman, and one slot spent on a franchise blueliner beats two slots spent on good players. Jean-Christophe now owns a top defenceman in Harley and a versatile winger in Hagel, so he isn't empty-handed. But I give the edge to Dennis for landing the one player in the deal who can anchor a roster.

On the waiver wire, Ducks on the Pond 🦆 spent $86 on Trevor Zegras, dropping Pyotr Kochetkov. Zegras delivered one assist, 4 shots, 2 hits and a block. Tom now has $11 remaining out of $100, and that's a lot of budget for a quiet week. If a goalie emergency hits, he's got fumes in the tank. The bid was loud, and the return was a whisper.

Meanwhile, Mackin' a Drai Hard bid $7 on Easton Cowan, dropping Jamie Drysdale, and got two goals, 9 shots, 4 hits and 3 blocks. Matthew has $75 left. League-wide, $93 was spent in total, and most of it was Tom's. Thirteen of the fourteen teams are still sitting on most of their budgets, and the rest of the league should be paying attention.

The Trade Board

by Gary "The Wire" Halloran

The biggest name on the board belongs to Oscar Meier Wiener. I'm told Dave is shopping Timo Meier and "others," and the source places the manager on a Mexican beach with a few Coronas while the calls go out. I can't confirm the beach. I can confirm the standings: his club sits second at 11-6-1, a legitimate contender, and contenders don't usually open the shop in mid-October. Either there's a hole elsewhere on the roster that Dave is trying to fill, or the front office senses the sellers' window is open. Keep an eye on this one.

Now to the Ducks on the Pond 🦆, where my phone has been busiest. Tom is reportedly shopping absolutely everyone. A source tells me the sales pitch has been delivered to the entire league, and I'm told the tone has not been subtle. The same source describes a full crash-out after Tom missed on a player in a trade, only for that player to be gifted to another team. I can't confirm who the player was. Further hearing: the beers are flowing, the offers keep coming, and the quality of those offers has not improved.

Context matters here. The Ducks are 4-12-2, 13th in the standings, with a roster built almost entirely from the team the club is named for. They just put in an $86 FAAB bid on Trevor Zegras, most of the league's spending this week. That is a front office in motion, and not one working from a plan I can identify.

The second tip adds a wrinkle. I'm told Tom is a "fake Ducks fan" who won't trade for Dostal. Lukas Dostal is an Anaheim goalie whose name could not fit the Ducks theme better, so a refusal to chase him would raise eyebrows. I can't confirm the motive. Perhaps Tom is protecting a price, or maybe he's simply done paying retail. Either way, the loyalty test is on.

Elsewhere, Mackin' a Drai Hard leads the league at 13-5-0 and keeps adding, with a $7 bid on Easton Cowan. Matthew is not resting on a lead. And Locks and Hagels (4-14-0), which landed Thomas Harley and Brandon Hagel, shipped Matthew Schaefer to Jacks in the Box. A last-place club moving a pair of names like that tells me Jean-Christophe is already thinking about next year.

The trade deadline is March 3, and for the sellers on this board the clock is already running. More to come, and I'm told there's a lot more.

That's the word from The Wire. The phones never stop.

The Fancy Stats Desk

by Dr. Ingrid Lindqvist

Hacklin Celeweenie scored 5 goals and 14 points, the lowest totals in the league in both, and still won its matchup 5-4-0. The skaters did their work elsewhere. Hacklin Celeweenie put up 100 shots on goal, one fewer than the league high of 101, and 52 hits against a league average of 46.86. Its goaltending delivered 4 wins, the league high, with a 1.73 GAA and a .933 save percentage. Only Jetting out of Winnipeg was better in those two. Offence was the only thing missing, and four wins elsewhere covered it.

The week could have been even kinder. Hacklin Celeweenie's 48 blocked shots were the second-highest total in the league. Only its opponent, Gavin Mackinaw, with 53, finished ahead. That gap cost a category. Gavin Mackinaw has its own complaint. Its 3 wins were tied for second in the league, with three other teams matching it, and only Hacklin Celeweenie's 4 beat them. Both managers left a category on the table that most weeks would have been theirs.

Jetting out of Winnipeg posted the best goaltending in the league: a 1.00 GAA and a .957 save percentage, against league averages of 2.75 and .893. It won only 5-4-0. The skaters scored 9 goals and 22 points, and its 20 blocks were the lowest total in the league. Goon City Grifters led the league with 39 points, built on 26 assists, and lost the week anyway. It took goals, assists, points and blocks, and dropped shots by a single one, 97 to 96, hits by one, 33 to 32, plus the three goalie categories. Dr. Lindqvist does not believe in luck. A save percentage that far above league average does fit a small sample, though, and the PDO tables will want a word next week.

Matchup context decided Oscar Meier Wiener's 8-1-0 result. Its numbers were solid: 12 goals, 29 points, 57 hits, a 2.02 GAA and a .917 save percentage. Locks and Hagels supplied the rest, finishing with the league's worst GAA at 4.21 and the worst save percentage at .833. A good week against a bad goalie line produces the widest margin on the board. No other team won more than six categories all week.
