Week 1 Recap (2026-09-29 — 2026-10-04)

Monday, October 5, 2026

Matchup Recaps

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

The defending champion went down hard. Jacks in the Box lost 7-2-0 to Mackin' a Drai Hard, and Dennis's three-peat bid opened with a thud. The goaltending was fine, with a 2.04 GAA and a .930 save percentage that took both rate categories. But Mackin' a Drai Hard won every skater category, plus wins at 2 to 1, behind 88 shots, 53 hits and 35 blocks. Matthew has never finished higher than third in this league, and he just beat the two-time defending champ by a landslide. That's a lunch-pail week from top to bottom.

Derek's 50 in '07 wasn't far behind, beating Goon City Grifters 7-1-1. Derek has been the runner-up twice and is still hunting a first title, and a week with a 1.50 GAA and a .941 save percentage is a decent way to start the hunt. Eleven goals and 21 assists did the rest. Goon City Grifters salvaged blocked shots, 29 to 23, and tied wins at two apiece. Ivan's club otherwise got steamrolled, and the blocks are the only honest category he can take home.

Then there's Gavin Mackinaw, a perfect example of what I distrust about winning on volume. Jesse's club took all six skater categories in a 6-2-1 win over Ducks on the Pond 🦆, with 82 shots, 49 hits and 43 blocks. It did that while posting a 4.40 GAA and a .861 save percentage, so Tom's goalies took both rate categories at 2.56 and .877 and still lost. The wins category tied at one each. Call it a rotten crease propped up by a very good blue line and a lot of bodies in the lane.

Jetting out of Winnipeg beat Calvin and Dobes 6-3-0 behind 24 assists and 94 shots, and Cole's goalies added a 2.03 GAA and a .927 save percentage. Jean-Christophe's club had the hits, 66 to 37, along with goals (11 to 9) and wins (3 to 1). That's a beauty of a physical effort, and it still lost a six-category decision. Hits are the most honest stat in the sport, but they don't win a week on their own.

The three 5-3-1 and 5-2-2 results were messier. On The Bench Hockey 2.0 beat Oscar Meier Wiener 5-3-1, taking five skater categories while goals tied at eight. Dave's return after nearly a decade away came with a 1.59 GAA, a .940 save percentage and four wins. His skaters just couldn't keep pace, and Jordan's goalies at 3.71 and .831 were no help.

Pasta La Vista beat Hackin Darts 5-3-1 behind 11 goals, 83 shots and 58 hits, with points tied at 23. Mac's crease was a dandy at 1.34 and .947, but Tyler's four wins to three and a 33-32 edge in blocks decided it.

Grab 'Em By The Bussi beat Nette's Sweaty Yetis 5-2-2 despite a 4.31 GAA and a .824 save percentage. Jim's skaters took five categories, 81 shots to 50, while assists and wins finished level. Andrew's goalies were stingy at 1.22 and .949, but the skaters couldn't give them anything to work with.

Players of the Week

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Elias Pettersson is the Skater of the Week, and he earned it the lunch-pail way. Calvin and Dobes got 2 goals and 4 assists for 6 points out of the Vancouver centre, plus 19 hits and 6 blocked shots on top of 7 shots on goal. Nineteen hits from a first-line centre is the kind of line that makes a lifer like me put down the coffee. Jean-Christophe gets a category-stuffer who punishes you everywhere, and I've got no suspicion to raise about that one, because he didn't win on shots alone. Evan Bouchard of EDM was the loudest of the runners-up for On The Bench Hockey 2.0: 4 goals, 4 assists, 8 points and 12 shots from the back end. That's a defenceman playing like a winger with a power-play quarterback's licence. Filip Hronek gave Gavin Mackinaw 5 assists and a beautiful 17 blocked shots. Seventeen. That's a man who lives in the shooting lane, and I love him for it. Kyle Connor rounds out the skater nods for Pasta La Vista with 3 goals, 2 assists and 14 shots from WPG.

The goalie crown goes to Joel Hofer of STL, and it's a short résumé with a perfect finish: 1 win, 25 saves on 25 shots, a 1.000 save percentage for 50 in '07. Hofer has spent years as a goalie waiting for the net to be his, and a clean sheet's worth of saves in a win is the way to start collecting. The workload wasn't enormous across the week, but a goalie can only stop what's fired at him, and he stopped all of it. Derek can take a bow for a ratio-saver that cost nothing in GAA or SV%.

The volume goalies get their due too. Jesper Wallstedt of MIN won twice for Hackin Darts, allowing 2 goals on 47 shots for a 1.01 GAA and a .957 save percentage. Jeremy Swayman of BOS was right behind for Oscar Meier Wiener: 2 wins, a 1.47 GAA and a .947 save percentage over 57 shots. Dave's other netminder, Igor Shesterkin of NYR, also won twice, stopping 71 of 76 for a 1.67 GAA and a .934 save percentage. Two of the three runners-up share the same fantasy roster, which makes Dave's crease the best-kept secret in the league. Welcome back after the decade away, pal.

Power Rankings

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Contenders

Four teams opened the season with wins and the look of a playoff club. Muzz has seen enough hockey to know one week proves little, but he knows a lunch-pail start when he sees one.

#1 50 in ‘07 — 7-1-1 (.833) — W, 7-1-1

Derek's crew won the regular season last year and picked up where it left off, taking seven of nine categories. Two runner-up finishes still sit on the mantel, and the way this opened, the third try might be the one.

#2 Mackin’ a Drai Hard — 7-2-0 (.778) — W, 7-2-0

Matthew has never finished higher than third in all these years, and a 7-2-0 opener makes for a heck of a first shift. Muzz is calling it a beauty of a start and waiting to see whether this club can hold up when the schedule turns nasty.

#3 Gavin Mackinaw — 6-2-1 (.722) — W, 6-2-1

Jesse reached the final as a rookie and shows no sophomore slump here. A 6-2-1 win is how a runner-up announces it wants a second crack at the title.

#4 Grab 'Em By The Bussi — 5-2-2 (.667) — W, 5-2-2

Jim collected two ties on the way to this one, which is the kind of grinding win that lives in the lunch-pail section of the standings. A two-time champion knows a win is a win, and Muzz respects it.

Solid

Good starts, but not yet the kind that scare anybody. Each of these teams has a clean first week and a lot of questions left to answer.

#5 Jetting out of Winnipeg — 6-3-0 (.667) — W, 6-3-0

Cole is in his third season and opened with a tidy 6-3-0 win. No ties and no drama, just a clean night's work from a manager who is clearly learning the league.

#6 On The Bench Hockey 2.0 — 5-3-1 (.611) — W, 5-3-1

Jordan eked out a 5-3-1 win in the second year of the franchise. The name suggests a team that spends plenty of time on the pine, so the lineup deserves a close watch.

#7 Pasta La Vista — 5-3-1 (.611) — W, 5-3-1

Tyler made the playoffs as a rookie and begins the encore with the same 5-3-1 result as the team above. Muzz will take the win and wait for more evidence before handing out any compliments.

Mediocre

The first four losses of the year landed here. Nobody is panicking yet, but the standings have noticed.

#8 Oscar Meier Wiener — 3-5-1 (.389) — L, 3-5-1

Dave is back after nearly ten years away, and the league welcomed him with a loss. It was a respectable 3-5-1 rather than a beating, so call it rust and not ruin.

#9 Hackin Darts — 3-5-1 (.389) — L, 3-5-1

Mac has finished 13th and last in recent memory and needs a quick turnaround. The 3-5-1 loss shows a team in the fight, just a step behind.

#10 Calvin and Dobes — 3-6-0 (.333) — L, 3-6-0

Jean-Christophe took a 3-6-0 loss in the opener. A manager with a third-place finish on the résumé knows how to climb back, but the climb starts now.

#11 Nette's Sweaty Yetis — 2-5-2 (.333) — L, 2-5-2

Andrew managed two ties on the way to a loss, so at least a couple of categories ended up on the right side of even. After last year's finish near the bottom, he will want more than moral victories.

Rebuilding

Three teams opened with the sort of results that have a manager pulling up the waiver wire at midnight. Muzz does not buy that the season is over, but the hole is real.

#12 Ducks on the Pond 🦆 — 2-6-1 (.278) — L, 2-6-1

Tom won the very first title in this league's history and opened with a 2-6-1 loss. The pond is looking a little choppy, and the original champion needs a response.

#13 Jacks in the Box — 2-7-0 (.222) — L, 2-7-0

The back-to-back defending champion went 2-7-0 in the opener, and Dennis's three-peat bid starts in the basement. Muzz covers the league without favourites, and a 2-7-0 loss gets the same treatment whoever suffers it.

#14 Goon City Grifters — 1-7-1 (.167) — L, 1-7-1

Ivan has finished in the top six in every one of his four seasons, which makes a 1-7-1 opener a real jolt. The grifters hold the bottom spot, and it is a long way back up from there.

Best Pickup of the Week

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Jacks in the Box takes this one, and Dennis can put the plaque wherever he likes. Luca Cagnoni of SJ put up a goal and an assist, six shots on net, a hit and five blocked shots. That is a defenceman filling the sheet from the blue line, and the five blocks are the kind of work Muzz respects. The Sharks blueliner cost nothing but a roster spot and returned points, shots and the honest categories all at once. Call him "Cagnoni the Cashier," because the till never stopped ringing.

Oscar Meier Wiener gets the silver for Jared Spurgeon of MIN, who matched Cagnoni's goal, assist, hit and five blocks and trailed only on shots, with two. Dave has been back in the league a week and is already scrounging smart. Spurgeon is a veteran defenceman who gives a team a steady, unglamorous shift every night. That's a lunch-pail pickup.

Bronze goes to Mackin' a Drai Hard for Jared McCann of SEA, the C,LW who picked up four assists and six shots. It's a good week, and the lack of a single hit or block keeps him off the top step. A forward who only shoots and passes is a fine player and a suspicious fantasy asset. Muzz doesn't trust anyone who wins on shots alone.

William Karlsson of VGK goes first, because Jetting out of Winnipeg's pickup of the week produced zero goals, zero assists, zero points, zero hits and zero blocks. Tyler Bertuzzi of CHI rounds out the shame for Nette's Sweaty Yetis, who got a goal, a point, a single shot and two hits for the trouble. That is a line that shows up on the sheet and does nothing for the matchup. Andrew's front office saw a name and bought it. The name did very little.

Goalie Stream of the Week

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Joey Daccord of SEA takes the crown, and Gavin Mackinaw earns the praise for grabbing him. The line speaks for itself: one win, a 1.00 GAA, 32 saves on 33 shots and a .970 save percentage. One goal allowed. That is a goalie who saw 33 shots coming and turned away all but one. In a format where one bad start can wreck GAA and SV% for the entire week, Jesse rolled the dice and the dice came up gold.

A stream like this is the riskiest move in the sport, and Muzz respects a manager who commits. Jesse reached the final as a rookie last year, and this is the kind of aggressive goalie management that gets a team there. Daccord took on 33 shots and let one through. Jesse deserves the pat on the back and a long look at the waiver wire next week.

Front Office Failures

by Murray "Muzz" Kowalchuk

Start with the hot potatoes, because someone in this league needs to learn that dropping a player does not make him disappear. Mackin' a Drai Hard cut Brock Boeser loose, and Oscar Meier Wiener caught him. Boeser then produced no points, no shots, one hit and one block. Fair enough, Matthew probably had it right on this one. The roast is for the whole transaction: two managers, two roster moves, and a net gain for the league of one hit and one block. That's a lot of paperwork for a rounding error.

Then there's Dennis himself. Jacks in the Box dropped Tyler Bertuzzi, and Nette's Sweaty Yetis picked him up and Bertuzzi finished the week with a goal and a point. Muzz covers this league as a neutral party and will say it plainly: when a player you cut ends the week on somebody else's scoresheet, the front office is on notice. One goal is hardly a catastrophe, and the Yetis are welcome to it. The champion's three-peat chase starts with not feeding the competition.

The Fancy Stats Desk

by Dr. Ingrid Lindqvist

The most reliable pattern this week is that elite goaltending lost matchups. Five teams posted a save percentage of .930 or better: Nette's Sweaty Yetis (.949), Hackin Darts (.947), 50 in '07 (.941), Oscar Meier Wiener (.940) and Jacks in the Box (.930). Only 50 in '07 turned it into a win, 7-1-1. The other four lost, against a league average of .902. All four of their opponents had goalies below that average: .824, .894, .831 and .886. The skaters decided those matchups.

Nette's Sweaty Yetis are the extreme case. They had the league's best GAA at 1.22 and the best save percentage at .949, and still lost 5-2-2 to Grab 'Em By The Bussi. Grab 'Em By The Bussi had a .824 save percentage, the league's lowest, and a 4.31 GAA. The Yetis posted 50 shots on goal and 16 points, both league lows. Their 6 goals matched Jacks in the Box for the league low. Two goalie categories can't carry a team that supplies almost nothing else.

Hackin Darts took a milder version of the same loss. A 1.34 GAA and a .947 save percentage earned them three categories, but Pasta La Vista won on goals (11 to 8), shots (83 to 77), hits, blocks and wins. Points finished tied at 23, which is the only reason the score reads 5-3-1. Oscar Meier Wiener tied Pasta La Vista for the league lead with 4 wins, posted a 1.59 GAA, and still dropped 5-3-1 to On The Bench Hockey 2.0. Their goalies produced exactly three categories, and their skaters were outshot 85 to 58.

The reverse case belongs to Gavin Mackinaw. Their 4.40 GAA is the league's worst, their .861 save percentage sits well under the league average, and they won 6-2-1 anyway. They led the league with 43 blocks, scored 10 goals and had 18 assists, while Ducks on the Pond managed 26 hits, the league's lowest. Goaltending this poor rarely holds up from week to week. Bad save percentages tend to drift back toward the average, and this week the skaters covered for it.
